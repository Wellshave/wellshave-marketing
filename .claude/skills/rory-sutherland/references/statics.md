# Static Studio — Image Ads

Read this whenever the work involves static/image ads, carousels, native image ads, or the image half of a
concept that also has video. Statics used to leave this studio and go to a designer with nothing but a mood;
they now leave with an idea. This file is the discipline.

A static is not a smaller video. It is a **poster**, and posters obey one law: the whole thing lands in a
glance, or it does not land. The entire ad is the hook — the words and the picture arrive simultaneously and
must say the same thing.

## The one law: one big idea, half a second

The failure mode of every static I have ever been shown is **addition**. Another benefit, another badge,
another burst, another line the brand manager wanted. Each addition feels free and none of them are: they
share out the half-second of attention until every element gets too little to register.

If an element does not serve the one idea, it is not neutral. Remove it.

Goal dilution applies to pictures exactly as it applies to products and arguments: one product, one job, one
claim, one idea.

## What a static has to do

1. **Stop the scroll.** It must look unlike the feed around it. Not prettier — *different*. A well-executed ad
   that follows every category convention is a well-executed way of being ignored.
2. **Show its proof.** Statics cannot argue, so they must demonstrate. Before/after, the mechanism visible,
   the guard against skin, a review screenshot, a number precise enough to be checked. Assertions are free and
   read as free; demonstration is costly and reads as true.
3. **Land one idea** for someone moving fast and reading nothing carefully.
4. **Not be boring.** Same test as everywhere: if it bores you on your own screen, it is boring.

## Headlines

- **Short main headline.** Nobody reads a long one on an image.
- If the idea needs more room, **split it**: a short headline that survives the glance, plus a sub-headline
  for whoever the glance recruited. The first hit stays scannable; the interested reader continues.
- The headline does one of the three jobs a hook does: call out who this is for, show the benefit, or open a
  gap. Same mechanisms as `references/scripts.md` — a static headline is a hook that has to work in silence.
- **Specificity beats superlatives**, and on a static it beats them twice over, because a precise number is
  the cheapest proof available. "90 dagen, geld terug" outperforms "tevredenheidsgarantie".

## Statics by awareness stage

Statics work across the whole range, which is precisely why so many of them are aimed at nobody. Choose:

| Stage | The static leads with |
|---|---|
| **Unaware** | A native-looking image that earns attention on its own, carrying the argument in long primary text. The picture is deliberately plain. |
| **Problem aware** | The problem, named precisely and kindly, then the mechanism |
| **Solution aware** | The desired outcome, or your solution positioned against the alternatives |
| **Product aware** | Proof, comparison, the objection killed on the image |
| **Most aware** | The offer. Here the offer *is* the creative |

Sophistication sets the claim level, exactly as in `scripts.md`: at stage 3 lead with the mechanism, at
stage 4 mechanism plus visible proof, at stage 5 stop selling the product and sell identity. In the NL/BE
grooming market that means a static making a bare "glad en huidvriendelijk" claim is not a weak ad, it is an
ad aimed at a market that stopped existing several years ago.

**The alignment check.** Copy and image must sit at the same awareness stage *and* the same sophistication
stage. A headline written for a first-timer over a photograph composed for a connoisseur is the most common
way a competent static fails, and it is invisible until you check for it deliberately.

## The batch rule (and how it differs from video)

For video we vary the door: 3 hooks, one body. For statics, the whole ad is the door, so we vary in two
distinct passes and never confuse them:

- **Visual pass** — one idea, one headline, **three visual treatments**. Same words, genuinely different
  images: different composition, different proof shot, different setting. This isolates the picture.
- **Angle pass** — 3 to 5 concepts on *different ideas*: different mechanism, different avatar call-out,
  different proof. This is where the real leverage lives.

Three colourways of one idea is not a test, it is a mood board. Vary the idea, or vary the picture, and know
which one you are doing.

## Static output format (Notion-compatible)

Match the Content Pieces info block so a static can be pasted into the same database as a script. The info
block is identical to the script format in `scripts.md`; only the creative block below differs.

**Info block**: 👤 Customer Persona · ❤️ Emotion/Desire · 👀 Awareness stage · 😣 Main problem · 🧠 Marketing
Angle · 💭 Messaging · 📈 Market sophistication · ⚙️ Mechanism & belief shift · 🛡️ Objection to kill ·
🧪 Test hypothesis & watch metric

(🎭 Casting applies only when a person appears in the image; note the messenger type if so.)

**Creative block**:

```
CONCEPT: <the one idea, one line>
Why it stops the scroll: <what makes it unlike the feed around it>

Main headline: "<short>"
Sub-headline (optional): "<the rest>"
Mechanism callout (optional): "<named on the image>"

VISUAL A: <what is literally shown — and where the PROOF is visible>
VISUAL B: <same words, different treatment>
VISUAL C: <same words, different treatment>

Supporting element: <badge, rating, guarantee — only if it serves the one idea>
Primary text: <short, or long-form native — say which and why>
```

**Removal note** (required, one line): what you deliberately left out, and why. It exists to make addition a
decision rather than a reflex.

Then offer 3–5 **angle** variations, each on a different mechanism, not a different palette.

## Working with the image tools

The strategy is the scarce part; the rendering is not. Decide the idea, the proof and the composition here,
then hand the visual brief to Eva (`eva-gpt-brand-fotograaf`) for on-brand prompts. Do not let the tooling
choose the idea — a beautiful render of nothing in particular is the most expensive kind of cheap ad.

## Review checklist

1. One idea, in half a second?
2. Different from the feed, or merely competent?
3. Headline short? Longer message split into a sub-headline?
4. Proof **visible**, not claimed?
5. Copy and image aligned on awareness *and* sophistication?
6. Overloaded — what comes out without losing the idea?
7. Who it is for: called out, or shown?
8. Is the audience large enough to be worth owning?
9. Boring?
