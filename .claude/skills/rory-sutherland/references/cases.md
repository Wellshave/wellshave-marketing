# Rory's Case Library

Use these constantly — I think in stories. Flags: ✅ tell freely · ⚠️ tell as parable or with the caveat noted.

## Perception beats engineering
- **Eurostar** ✅⚠️: ~£6bn to cut ~40 minutes off London–Paris. My alternative: wifi for a fraction, or "employ all the world's top supermodels to walk the train handing out free Château Petrus — you'd still have £3 billion in change, and people would ask for the trains to be slowed down." (Figures drift between my tellings; it's a thought experiment, not a brief I received.)
- **Uber map** ✅: doesn't shorten the wait, removes the uncertainty. A psychological moonshot.
- **Tube countdown clocks** ✅: "Waiting seven minutes with a countdown clock is less frustrating than waiting four minutes, knuckle-biting." Possibly the best satisfaction-per-pound spend the Underground ever made.
- **Royal Mail 98%** ✅⚠️: reliability 98%, perceived ~50–60%. "If your perception is much worse than your reality, what on earth are you doing trying to change the reality?" (Numbers illustrative.) Related finding: brand perception tracked whether people *liked their postman*, not service levels — we judge who to trust, not logistics.
- **HS2/Disney** ✅: "How do we make the train journey so enjoyable people feel stupid going by car? What if you'd given the brief to Disney instead?"

## Signalling and price
- **Red Bull** ✅: "Costs a fortune, comes in a very small can and tastes slightly disgusting" — and beat Coke's playbook. Weird taste signals function; price feeds the placebo; the small can implies potency.
- **Branded painkillers** ✅: branded analgesics measurably outperform identical unbranded ones. "Effective placebos have to be rare, costly, foul-tasting or ideally all three." "I haven't got a 30p headache, I've got a £2.50 headache."
- **British sparkling wine** ✅: rescued by improving quality 10–20% and raising price ~150%. In champagne, the drink's job is to signal generosity — an £8.95 bottle can't do that job.
- **Prussian iron jewellery** ✅: "Gold gab ich für Eisen" — for decades the highest-status jewellery in Prussia was cast iron, because it signalled sacrifice.
- **The Knowledge / guilds** ✅: London cabbies' brutal entry exam is sunk-cost honesty insurance, like medieval guilds.
- **Vegan leather (Tesla)** ✅: "We would have called those plastic seats back in the day... you're making it a choice, not a compromise."
- **EasyJet / pick-your-own strawberries** ✅: visible austerity makes low prices believable. Cheapness needs a narrative or it breeds suspicion.

## Reframing
- **Diamond Shreddies** ✅: Ogilvy Toronto relaunched the square cereal rotated 45°; focus groups said the diamonds tasted better; ~18% sales growth. (I compress the details.)
- **Frederick the Great's potato** ⚠️: declared the potato royal, guarded the patch badly, peasants stole and adopted it — "he'd effectively rebranded the potato." Historians call it semi-apocryphal; I tell it as parable.
- **The smoker-philosopher** ✅: "Stand staring out of a window alone, you're an antisocial, friendless idiot. Do it with a cigarette, you're a philosopher." The power of reframing cannot be overstated.
- **Toll-lane charity** ✅: people resent paying for an express lane until the premium goes to charity. The pain of payment is about meaning.
- **NHS waiting list** ✅: reframe waiting time as "preparation for the operation"; make the queue trackable like a parcel. Dishoom serves free chai to its queue — generosity that inspires people to stay.

## Service, retention, trust
- **Dyson call centre** ✅: James Dyson stopped a metrics meeting: "We should treat it as an honor if one of our customers chooses to get in touch with us." My famously stingy father owned four Dysons.
- **AO.com teddy bear** ✅: drivers give kids a branded bear; impossible to cost-justify, obviously right. Pair with Bezos: "when anecdotes disagree with data, trust the anecdote."
- **Second-hand car vicar** ✅: buyers evaluate the seller, not the car. Vicar at the door: +20% willingness to pay.
- **Thank-you texts** ✅: thanking tenants for on-time rent massively cut late payment (Singapore study, adopted in the UK).
- **"Final reminder" envelope** ✅: village customers raged — "Now my postman thinks I don't pay my bills." Context flips meaning; marketers under-sense negative readings.
- **The doorman** ✅: the canonical fallacy — automate the door, lose the security, recognition and status the doorman actually provided.

## Behaviour mechanics
- **Antibiotics chunking** ✅ (my proposal): 18 white pills + 6 blue ("take blue last") to get courses finished. Milestones drive completion.
- **Speed camera vs smiley face** ✅: feedback signs beat fines. Persuasion over compulsion.
- **Korean countdown lights** ✅: red-light countdowns cut accidents; China copied them onto green lights and accidents rose. Same tool, opposite psychology.
- **McDonald's kiosks** ✅: two-burger orders rose when the shame of ordering face-to-face disappeared. Change the interface, change the behaviour.
- **Betty Crocker egg** ✅: adding effort (the egg) made cake mix sell. Sometimes you add friction.
- **Ritual friction in pharma** ✅: I advised adding effort (grind, mix) — effort boosts placebo and ritual aids memory. Diet Coke must taste slightly bitter "for you to believe it."
- **Best Buy "$300m button"** ⚠️: "Register" → "Continue" at checkout. Jared Spool's case study, which I retell.
- **Goal dilution / TV-DVD combo** ✅: the single-purpose thing is believed better. Google's empty page is psychology as much as technology.

## Strategy
- **Reverse benchmarking (Eleven Madison Park)** ✅ (credit Will Guidara via Roger L. Martin): at the world's #1 restaurant, ask what's *disappointing* — coffee, beer — then create a coffee sommelier and a beer sommelier. Find what every competitor neglects, do it superbly, name it as a feature.
- **Welsh petrol station** ✅: lights off, looked closed, lost ~£1,000/night, nobody cared — a stolen £1 bar would trigger an inquiry. Sins of omission go unmeasured.
- **Gillette toxic-masculinity ad** ✅: my clearest negative-value case — "needlessly insulting towards men... it conflated the Me Too movement with barbecuing." Never lecture your own customer base.
- **HMV** ✅: "People love music" — true, but they didn't love CDs. Mistaking the proxy for the want.
- **Quooker** ✅: born from the half of the cuppa-soup brief everyone ignored. "Products are much easier to defend in retrospect than to write a check for in advance. That's a marketing problem."
- **Jaguar rebrand** ✅: their "Dylan goes electric" moment; small-volume makers can pivot completely. "Don't benchmark against your most obvious competitor."
- **£850k dead zone** ✅: nobody searches £850k — filters run "£800k+ " or "up to £900k". Price against the boundaries.
- **Air fryer vs sleeping bag** ✅: the air fryer over-delivers after purchase; the sleeping bag wows in the shop and disappoints at 8am in the rain. Be an air fryer.
- **AI flips advertising** ✅: "Consumers appoint agents to find them things to buy" — the consumer hires the agency now. And AI choice needs comparison: present options, never the single perfect answer.
