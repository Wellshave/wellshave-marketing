# Primary Text (the copy above the creative)

## How much it matters

Be honest about the weighting: **primary text is roughly a 20% lever. The creative itself is the other 80%.**
Most performance from testing new ads comes from what people *see*. Do not let a user spend their week
rewriting primary text when the creative is the problem.

**The exception is native ads**, where the long-form primary text *is* the creative and the image is
incidental. There the weighting inverts.

## Length follows format and awareness

- **Video ads:** keep primary text **short**. The video does the selling; the copy only has to catch the right
  person and support it.
- **Static ads:** the copy carries more weight, so it can run longer.
- **Native ads:** deliberately very long (roughly 2,000–2,500 characters), opening with a short visible line
  that earns the "see more" click, then unfolding into a full story. It should read like something a person
  posted organically, not like an ad.

## Primary text by awareness stage

Write the copy at the same stage as the creative. The shapes he uses:

- **Most aware:** the offer enhancer, and nothing else. These people know and trust you and are waiting for a
  reason to act. No problem set-up, no solution explanation, none of it is needed.
- **Product aware (USP):** what makes this product unique, concretely. Ingredients, specs, what it does, in
  plain terms.
- **Product aware (testimonial):** open in a customer's voice, then bridge into the product.
- **Product aware (authority):** lead with the credible figure. Keep it short if the video is doing the work.
- **Solution aware:** acknowledge they have tried a pile of solutions, dismiss the need for them, introduce
  yours, give the benefits, add social proof, close with a call to action. This one often runs the full sales
  sequence inside the copy.
- **Problem aware:** call out the tribe and the problem in their own words, promise the outcome, name the
  solution, close.
- **Unaware:** the native long-form story.

## When to write new primary text vs reuse the winner

The decision rule is simple, and it is the same test used everywhere in this system:

> **Does this new creative hit the same awareness stage, avatar and desire as the ads already running?**

- **Yes** (it is an iteration of an existing winner) → **copy and paste the best-performing primary text.**
  Keep it simple, do not create work.
- **No** (new avatar, new desire, new awareness stage, a seasonal or holiday push) → **write fresh primary
  text for it.** Pairing a new-segment creative with the old winner's copy is an alignment mismatch, exactly
  like a copy-visual mismatch.

## Testing new primary text against a winner

When creative learnings suggest the copy on a top ad can be beaten:

1. Duplicate the existing ad and rename it clearly as a primary-text test.
2. Turn off the platform's auto-enhancement suggestions.
3. Swap in the new primary text, leaving the creative untouched.
4. Launch it into the same CBO and let the algorithm decide. It either takes spend and beats the original or
   it does not.

Because only the copy changed, the read is clean.

## Finding copy inspiration

Same rule as hooks: **never take copy from a direct competitor.** Pull from currently-scaling ads in
*unrelated* niches, filtered for active status, growing brands, your country and language, and high ad rank.
Read for what grabs *you*, then rebuild the structure for your product and offer, correcting anything that
does not match your actual offer or claims.

## Quality bar

- **A fourth-grader should understand it.** A confused mind says no. Deliberately dumbing the language down is
  a real technique here, not a figure of speech.
- **One big idea**, same as the creative.
- **Every word intentional.** In his highest-spending ads, each line does a job: call out the tribe, open
  curiosity, name the problem, twist the knife, answer the objection, deliver social proof, explain the
  mechanism simply, then the call to action. Nothing is filler.
- **Not spammy.** Copy that reads like a discount blast performs worse than copy that reads like a person.
- **Not boring.**
