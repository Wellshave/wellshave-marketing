# Landing Pages & Destination Match

A landing page is **anything you send traffic to**. That includes the product page. There are four types, and
picking the right one is an extension of the awareness decision, not a separate discipline.

He is currently sending roughly 90% of traffic to **listicles**, which are outperforming advertorials and
nearly all product pages in his accounts.

## The four destinations

**Advertorial.** Reads like an article: a personal story, everything the writer tried, why it failed, then the
new solution. Best for **unaware and problem-aware** audiences, and for diagnosis-style markets. Do not point
an advertorial at a simple, obvious product; there is nothing to diagnose.

**Listicle.** "N reasons why…". Lets you control exactly which points the reader absorbs before reaching the
product page, which makes it the natural home for your USPs and your us-versus-them comparisons. Best for
**solution-aware and product-aware** audiences: either introducing a new solution or arguing why yours beats
the identical-looking alternatives. This is his current default.

**Quiz funnel.** The reader answers questions and self-diagnoses. Best for **unaware to problem-aware**: the
ad plants the problem, the quiz confirms it, and the product is positioned as the answer to the diagnosis the
reader just gave themselves.

**Product page (PDP).** For **product-aware and most-aware** traffic, and for detailed proof-heavy ads that
have already done the selling.

## Why the destination changes performance

Two mechanisms, and both matter for how you brief the page:

1. **Congruence for the reader.** A story-led ad for a new avatar landing on a generic product page is a
   mismatch one click later. The reader loses the thread.
2. **Congruence for the algorithm.** The platform scans one level deep: it reads your creative *and* the page
   you send to, and uses both to decide who to show the ad to. Aligned creative and page give it a clear,
   consistent signal about who this is for. A creative built for a new tribe pointing at a page built for the
   old mass market sends mixed signals, and the algorithm struggles to find the right people.

In one worked example, moving from PDP to a listicle cut cost-per-purchase from roughly $85 to $62. Conversion
rate barely moved; **CPM and cost-per-link-click fell**, which is the algorithmic-congruence effect rather
than a persuasion effect.

**So: when you test a new avatar, build the landing page for that avatar too.** The creative alone is half the
signal.

## Building pages

The practical route he uses: find a currently-scaling page in an *unrelated* niche as your structural
inspiration, keep a running inspiration file of pages you like, then have a model rebuild that **structure**
for your product using your own survey data, reviews and product page as input, exported as HTML. Iterate by
screenshotting the result and directing changes conversationally, the way you would brief a designer. Host it
either on a third-party page builder or a Shopify page app.

Two things that trip people up:
- **Images and video must be hosted** (upload to the store and use those URLs).
- **The pixel must be installed on the custom page.** It does track correctly once it is there; custom domains
  in the ad account are not a problem in his experience.

**Never structurally copy a direct competitor's page.** Same rule as hooks and copy.

## Testing a landing page

Mechanically simple: duplicate the ad set, keep the creatives identical, change only the URL. Or duplicate the
ads within one ad set and point each at a different destination. Either is fine, as long as everything stays
inside the same CBO so the algorithm allocates spend between them.

Test two to four destinations against the same creative: listicle, advertorial, quiz, PDP.

## When to reach for a landing page test

The diagnostic split is the important part:

- **Bad cost-per-link-click** → the creative is the problem. Fix the creative first.
- **Good cost-per-link-click but bad CPA** → the creative is doing its job and the **page or offer** is the
  bottleneck. This is the signal to test a new destination.

This is also the fix for a promising new-message ad that takes spend at a bad CPA: the creative found the
people, and the page is not built for them.

Model the upside honestly before promising anything: run a conservative case (say a 10% conversion-rate lift)
and an aggressive one (20%) against current spend, AOV and cost-per-link-click, so expectations are realistic.

## Internal workflow

Track landing pages exactly like creatives: an idea, a hypothesis, and recorded learnings after the test, in
the same board and sheet as creative work. Pages compound the same way creatives do.
