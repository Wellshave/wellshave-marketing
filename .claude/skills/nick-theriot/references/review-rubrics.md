# Review Rubrics

How to critique creative the way Theriot would: score against the system, be specific, rewrite the weak parts.
Never vague praise, it helps nobody. Always end with concrete before/after fixes.

## How to present a review

1. **Verdict in one line** — will this take spend and hold, or not, and the single biggest reason.
2. **Scorecard** — rate each criterion (✅ strong / ⚠️ weak / ❌ broken) with one sentence of evidence.
3. **The one to three highest-leverage fixes**, with **before → after** rewrites.
4. **A rewritten version or redesigned concept.** Do not just diagnose, show the fix.

If the core idea is wrong, say so and propose a different angle rather than polishing a dead concept.

## The master rubric: the eight traits of ads that scale

This is his own list, drawn from the ads that have each spent six figures. Use it as the top-level scorecard
for any creative, video or static. Everything else in this file is detail underneath it.

1. **A visual hook that grabs attention.** Something the viewer has not seen before. If people have seen it a
   hundred times, your version will not work. It must fire dopamine *and* catch the right person.
2. **Built for a large audience.** The call-out, problem and desire must be shared by a large group. Ads built
   for narrow, poetic, or over-qualified segments do not spend. Build with top-of-funnel intent.
3. **Intention behind every word.** Each line does a job: call out the tribe, open curiosity, raise the stake,
   name the problem, answer the objection, prove it, explain the mechanism simply, then the call to action.
   Nothing is filler and nothing is there because a model generated it.
4. **Built to close on this ad alone.** Assume this is the only ad they will see. Awareness of the problem,
   why it matters, how the product works, proof, testimonials, and a CTA should all be present. In practice
   people see several ads before buying, but ads *built* to close single-handedly are the ones that spend big.
5. **Explains the product very simply.** A confused mind says no. A fourth-grader should follow it. Rewriting
   the ad in deliberately simpler language is a real step, not a metaphor. One big idea only.
6. **It is different.** Different visual, avatar, mechanism, or creative style than everyone else selling
   something similar. This is the recurring cause of ads that fail to spend.
7. **It is relevant right now.** Tied to what the audience is experiencing at this moment: the season, the
   holiday, the weather, the deadline they feel. Relevance is what moves a desire from "must be nice" to
   urgent. See the intensity section in `research-personas-and-desire.md`.
8. **It is not boring.** Subjective but reliable: if it feels boring to you when you watch or read it, it is
   boring and it will not convert. Two UGC videos with the same brief can differ 500x in spend purely on
   energy, pacing and whether the creator conveys the feeling the customer wants to have.

## Video script rubric

Every "no" is a fix.

1. **Every second earns the next?** Where is the dead beat that loses viewers? Never assume full watch.
2. **Hook:** ideal customer + curiosity + large TAM + something different? Does it **show** the desired result
   rather than claim it?
3. **Visual hook:** does the opening frame work with the sound off? Are there three genuinely different visual
   openings, or only one?
4. **Awareness match:** is the directness right for the stage? (Unaware and stage-5 ads must not open with the
   product, or even the problem.)
5. **Sophistication match:** is the framing right (direct claim / escalation / new mechanism / refined
   mechanism / identity)?
6. **Copy-visual alignment:** do words and visuals speak to the same awareness **and** sophistication stage?
   Does the casting and setting match the tribe?
7. **Core message:** if the sound were off, what would the viewer walk away thinking? Is it one thing?
8. **Mechanism:** can the viewer picture *how* it works differently from what they have tried?
9. **Proof:** visible, or just adjectives?
10. **Desire:** actively built, with the ultimate desire future-paced, or does the middle sag? Is the emotion
    **created** rather than asked about?
11. **Objections:** are the real ones named and dissolved, preferably by showing?
12. **CTA:** specific, matched to offer and destination?
13. **Destination match:** does the landing page fit this ad's awareness stage and avatar?
14. **Batch potential:** can this become one idea + three visual hooks, and then style variations? If it is a
    one-off, flag it.
15. **Compliance:** claims true and substantiated? Any borrowed authority, news framing, or restricted props
    that could get the account limited?

## Static ad rubric

Use the checklist in `static-ads.md` as scored criteria:

1. One big idea, graspable in half a second?
2. Stands out, or blends into the feed?
3. Headline short? Longer message split into a sub-headline?
4. Proof visible, not merely claimed?
5. Awareness match?
6. Sophistication match?
7. Copy-image alignment?
8. Overloaded? What can be removed without losing the idea?
9. Avatar clearly called out, or result clearly shown?
10. TAM large enough?
11. Boring?

## Primary text rubric

1. Right length for the format (short for video, longer for static, very long for native)?
2. Written at the same awareness stage, avatar and desire as the creative?
3. If it is an iteration of a winner, is it just reusing the proven copy (as it should)? If it is new
   messaging, has fresh copy been written for it (as it should)?
4. One big idea, fourth-grade readable, every word intentional?
5. Does it read like a person rather than a discount blast?

## Account-level diagnosis

Run this when the complaint is "my ads are not working" rather than "review this ad". Full detail in
`testing-and-iteration.md`.

1. **Not getting spend?** Creative first: boring, not different, too small a TAM, not a real challenger to the
   current top spender, wrong funnel stage, no emotional spike.
2. **Spending with bad CPA?** Split on cost-per-link-click: bad = creative, good = landing page or offer.
3. **Plateaued?** New avatar → new desire → new mechanism → new creative style → geo expansion → product
   expansion.
4. **Is it even an ad problem?** No unique mechanism, unrealistic required ROAS for the category, broken site
   or fulfilment, or a business needing rescue rather than scale. Say it plainly.
5. **Is there a learnings log?** If tests are not being marked successful or unsuccessful with recorded
   reasons, that is the real bottleneck: they are testing without compounding.
