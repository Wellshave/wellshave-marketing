# Market Awareness & Market Sophistication

The two psychology pillars, taken from Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising* and applied to
e-commerce. Theriot is explicit that this book is where his messaging methodology comes from. Awareness
decides **how directly you may speak**; sophistication decides **what kind of claim still works**.

Get these two right and the hook, script and static mostly write themselves. Get them wrong and no amount of
polish saves the creative.

## The five stages of market awareness

Awareness = how much the prospect already knows. Less aware means a bigger audience and more room to scale,
but the message has to be more indirect and story-led.

| Stage | Where the prospect is | What the creative does |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Doesn't know they have the problem. Largest audience. | Do not lead with the product or even the problem. Lead with something that earns the watch: a question, a comparison, a story, a spectacle. |
| **Problem aware** | Feels the pain, has no solution in mind. | Name the problem sharply, in their words. Prove you understand it. Diagnosis works well here. |
| **Solution aware** | Knows solutions exist, is forming a preference. | Call out the desired outcome, **or** lead with your solution/product itself if the market is sophisticated. |
| **Product aware** | Knows your product type. | Why you: proof, comparison, objections removed, unique benefit. |
| **Most aware** | Knows and trusts you. | Offer, urgency, deal, last chance. |

**Why this is the scaling unlock.** Most advertisers only speak to product-aware and most-aware buyers, which
is a small pool, so they hit a ceiling fast. Deliberately building unaware and problem-aware creative opens a
much larger audience, and audience size is what lets an account spend multiples more, profitably.

**A note on solution aware.** He went back to the book on this one, because people muddle it. Schwartz frames
it as *introducing new products*: in the headline you either **call out the desire** ("how to lose weight") or
**call out the solution/product itself** ("new wonder drug for losing weight"). Which one you pick is really a
sophistication decision: the desire-led headline suits a less sophisticated market, the solution-led headline
suits a more sophisticated one.

**The unaware opening, in practice.** The strongest unaware ads do not sell the product in the hook, they
**sell the reason to keep watching**. A worked example he praises: a cat-toy ad opens by asking who has a
better life, cats or dogs. It then compares the two, uses the comparison to surface a problem (cats are
under-stimulated, with a supporting statistic), sets up why existing toys fail, and only then introduces the
product. The ad is several beats deep before it looks like an ad.

## The five stages of market sophistication

Sophistication = how many claims this market has already heard. The more claim-tired, the more you must
**differentiate** rather than shout louder. Most e-commerce niches sit at stage 3–5.

| Stage | What you do | When |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Direct claim** | State the claim plainly. | You are first; virgin market. |
| **2. Bigger / faster claim** | Same claim, escalated and more specific. | Competitors arrive with the same claim. |
| **3. New mechanism** | Introduce the *how*: a new method, ingredient, technology. | The market no longer believes bare claims. |
| **4. Refine the mechanism** | Make your mechanism superior; product-vs-product. | Everyone now has a mechanism. |
| **5. Identity** | Sell identity, tribe, experience, who they want to be. | Market is fully sceptical and saturated. |

**Stage 5 behaves like unaware.** He states this directly: you open a stage-5 sophisticated market the same
way you open an unaware market. Do not sell the product in the hook, sell the reason to watch.

### Choosing your stage

1. Write your bare product claim.
2. Search that claim in the Meta Ads Library.
3. Count how many competitors make the same claim, and note which stage they are operating at.
4. **Crowded → move one stage ahead of them. Empty → stay at stage 1**, because a direct claim is enough.
5. Build the creative at the chosen stage.

Stay exactly **one step ahead**: never behind (you sound like everyone), never needlessly complex (if a direct
claim still works, use it).

### What each stage sounds like in a hook

- **Stage 2 (escalate):** everyone says "lose fat", you say "lose 18 lbs in 4 weeks". Specificity and size are
  what make the choice obvious.
- **Stage 3 (new mechanism):** name the *how* and pair it with a superiority claim, e.g. a new laser treatment
  that clears acne in 30 minutes. He also builds hooks by asking a **question based on how the mechanism
  works**: what would happen to your acne if the glands feeding it were shut down for a few days? That reframes
  a tired market as a new opportunity.
- **Stage 4 (refine):** everyone sells the same product, so you claim the better version of it, e.g. a 12-hour
  shift with zero pain and no break-in period.
- **Stage 5 (identity):** the tribe brand from `research-personas-and-desire.md`.

## The alignment check (a classic killer)

**Copy and visual must speak to the same awareness stage AND the same sophistication stage.** A hook written
for a beginner over footage aimed at someone who has already tried three competing products will fail, and so
will the reverse. Check this explicitly on every concept and in every review.

Extend the check to the **destination**: an unaware, story-led ad pointing at a bare product page is the same
mismatch one click later. See `landing-pages.md`.

## Using the two grids together

Plot the audience on both axes before writing anything, and state them at the top of every deliverable:

- **Unaware + stage 5** → question/comparison/spectacle opening, story-led, product late, advertorial or quiz
  destination.
- **Problem aware + stage 3** → name the problem, introduce the new mechanism, listicle or advertorial.
- **Solution aware + stage 4** → lead with your solution and why it beats the alternatives; listicle.
- **Product aware + stage 4** → proof, comparison, objection removal; product page.
- **Most aware, any stage** → offer, urgency, ending-soon; product page.

His 80/20 rule applies here: 80% of creative doubles down on the awareness/sophistication combination already
working, 20% deliberately tests a **new** awareness stage, sophistication stage, avatar or desire.
