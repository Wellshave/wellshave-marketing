# Video Scripts

How to build a video ad script. The spine is always **hook → build desire → mechanism → proof → objections →
CTA**, serving ONE core message, matched to the audience's awareness stage and the market's sophistication
stage.

## What "core message" means

Before writing a line, name the core message: **what the viewer walks away thinking after watching.** Not the
script, not the features. If the sound were off and they only saw the visuals, what idea would land?

A useful test: a fifteen-second silent video of people in a gym doing explosive lifts while drinking something
communicates "this gives you energy" and simultaneously communicates *who it is for*. Swap the cast from
competitive bodybuilders to yoga mothers and the core message changes entirely, with no change to the script.
The casting, setting and styling are part of the message, not decoration.

## The script spine

- **Hook (0–3s):** appeals to the ideal customer, intrigues curiosity, large TAM, says or shows something
  different. See `hooks.md`.
- **Build desire:** make them want the outcome, in their own words, with emotion. Future-pace the ultimate
  desire, not just the functional one.
- **Mechanism:** explain the *how* vividly enough that the viewer can picture why it works differently from
  what they have tried. The form scales with sophistication (direct claim → new mechanism → refined mechanism
  → identity).
- **Proof:** demonstrate, do not assert. Visible proof beats every adjective.
- **Objections:** name the real reasons not to buy and dissolve them, ideally by showing.
- **CTA:** specific, tied to the offer and the destination.

The standard: **every second must earn the next.** Never assume anyone watches to the end.

## Structure by awareness stage

- **Unaware:** do not open with the product *or* the problem. Open with something that earns the watch: a
  question, a comparison, a spectacle, a story. Then surface the problem, then why existing solutions fail,
  then the mechanism, then the product, then the offer. Harder to write, but it opens the largest audience,
  and it is what unlocks the big spending days. **Stage-5 sophistication uses this same shape.**
- **Problem aware:** open on the problem sharply, in their language. Diagnosis structures work well: list
  recognisable symptoms, assert a common deeper cause, introduce the mechanism.
- **Solution aware:** open on the desired outcome, or lead with the solution itself if the market is
  sophisticated. Position your type of solution and differentiate the mechanism.
- **Product aware:** open with why-you. Proof, comparison, objections removed, the superiority claim.
- **Most aware:** open with the offer and urgency.

## Storytelling that holds the middle

Structure the narrative with **"but" and "so"** rather than "and then". "And then" is flat; **but** injects
conflict and **so** injects consequence, which is what keeps emotion and watch time up through the middle of
the script where most ads sag.

Two structural moves that reliably hold attention:

- **The unexpected twist.** Break the pitch rhythm. Admitting a first version failed, or that you were wrong
  about something, is more engaging than uninterrupted praise, because it is not what every other ad does.
- **The origin story as authority.** Time and effort spent studying the problem earns the right to claim the
  solution.

## Desire amplifiers

After the hook, intensify desire before asking for anything. Stack a few, do not list them all: future-pacing
the ultimate outcome, sensory detail, social proof and belonging, status and identity, the cost of the status
quo continuing, ease and speed, oddly specific numbers, contrast with the painful alternative, authority
endorsement, risk reversal.

Remember the emotional rule from `hooks.md`: **create the feeling, then solve it.** Do not ask the viewer
whether they feel something.

## Objection handling

People do not buy because of specific, usually unspoken objections: price, "will this work for *me*", trust,
effort, time, and practical friction. Surface the real ones and answer them inside the script, preferably by
showing. In the cat-toy example, the objection ("will it work on my floors?") is dissolved in a single line
mid-demonstration rather than being saved for a FAQ.

Where price is the objection, remember: expensive usually means **under-justified**. Answer it by showing what
justifies it, not by apologising for it.

## The deliverable format

Always output something that can actually be shot. One ad idea = one body + one text hook + **three visual
hooks**.

```
CONCEPT: <one line>
Avatar: <the tribe>            Desire: <functional → ultimate>
Awareness: <stage>             Sophistication: <stage>
Differentiation: <which of the five>
Core message: <what they walk away thinking>
Format: <UGC / B-roll+music / B-roll+VO / animation / founder / native / street interview>
Destination: <advertorial / listicle / quiz / PDP>

[0-3s] HOOK  (text hook — identical across all three variants)
  Spoken/Text: "<line>"
  On-screen text: "<overlay>"
  VISUAL HOOK A: <what is literally on screen>
  VISUAL HOOK B: <a genuinely different opening>
  VISUAL HOOK C: <a third genuinely different opening>

[3-Xs] BUILD DESIRE
  Spoken: "..."      Visual: ...      ⮑ amplifier used: <which>

[ ] MECHANISM
  Spoken: "..."      Visual: <how the how is shown>

[ ] PROOF
  Spoken: "..."      Visual: <the proof, visible>

[ ] OBJECTIONS
  Objection: <the real one>   →   Answered by: <shown how>

[ ] CTA
  Spoken: "..."      Visual: <offer on screen>

Notes: camera angles, lighting, music/tone, casting.
```

Then offer **variations that change creative style** (same message rebuilt as B-roll+music, as animation, as
native long-form text, as a static) for messages already proven.

Keep the three levers distinct, as set out in SKILL.md: **iteration** (visual hook, person, location, format,
same message), **variation** (creative style), **new messaging** (new avatar, desire, awareness or
sophistication stage).

## Compliance while writing

Keep claims true and substantiated. Push the exaggeration into visuals, specificity and framing. Avoid
implying professional authority you do not have (there is a real difference between quoting a professional and
speaking *as* one), avoid real news branding when using a news-style frame, and avoid props or subjects the
platform restricts.
