# Testing, Iteration & Diagnosis

Creative strategy does not stop at the script. How creatives are batched, launched, judged and learned from is
what turns one good idea into a scaling account. His backend is deliberately simple, and knowing it makes the
creative advice sharper.

## The account structure

- **One CBO campaign per business goal.** One per product or product category, one per country. Some accounts
  run a single campaign, some run ten. There is **no separate testing campaign and no separate scaling
  campaign**: you test in the same campaign you scale.
- **Broad targeting** inside it. Advantage+ audience, minimal constraints, let the platform find people.
- **One ad idea per ad set**, with the **three creatives** of that batch inside it (same body, same text hook,
  three visual hooks).
- Upload as three separate ads. Dynamic/flexible formats make no meaningful difference.
- **Whitelisting**: one ad set per creator, still inside the main CBO.
- **Landing page tests**: duplicate the ad set, change only the URL, same CBO.

A new-product launch from scratch is the exception: there, a fresh CBO for the new avatar thesis is
appropriate, because the whole hypothesis is that avatar.

## The batch production system

The rhythm his team runs:

- **Five ad ideas per week, three creatives per idea.**
- Each idea is documented before anything is produced: the **idea**, the **hypothesis**, the **copy/script**,
  and the **visual hook variations**.
- Ideas are logged in a sheet, tasks tracked on a board with columns for to-do, doing, ready to launch, and
  post-launch, covering **both creatives and landing pages**.
- Production runs roughly a **two-week cycle**: one week for the strategist to write the ideas, one week for
  editors and designers to build them.
- Because of that lag, **the moment a batch goes live, start building the next batch.** Do not wait for
  results. The ideas that did not make this cut become the next round.

Ads are routinely **assembled from mixed sources**: some real UGC clips, some generated footage, some existing
library assets. That is normal, not a compromise.

## Width and depth

A useful frame he endorses, mapped onto how he actually works:

- **Width** = creative diversity for one core message. Take a proven message and spread it across formats:
  UGC, B-roll with music, B-roll with voiceover, animation, native long-form, static. Same message, more ways
  in, so it resonates with more people.
- **Depth** = going deeper on one core message over time. The longer you advertise a message, the better you
  understand it and the more precisely you can express it. Your version of a message six months from now will
  be sharper than today's, purely from accumulated learning.

For a genuinely **new** message, pick two or three creative styles to test it with. If it works, scale it and
begin producing variations. If not, drop it and move on.

## The 80/20 rule

- **80%** of creatives, time and production budget: double down on the core message that is already working,
  with iterations and variations.
- **20%**: genuinely new messaging, meaning **new desires, new avatars, new awareness stages, new
  sophistication stages.**

This is the single most useful planning number in the system. When a user asks "what should I make next
week?", split their batch this way.

## What happens after a winner

When a new message wins, do **not** immediately chase another new message. Mine it:

1. **Iterate the same ad**: swap the person on camera (different demographics), the location, the setting.
2. **Vary the style**: rebuild the same message as long-form native text, as B-roll with voiceover, as
   animation, as a static.
3. If an *iteration* then wins, keep iterating off the **original winning message**, not off the iteration.
4. Goal throughout: lower CPA or raise ROAS enough to justify raising budget.

A strong message has dozens of lives. Do not retire it, spin it.

## Judging a test

- Let a new ad set run about **seven days**.
- The verdict is binary and gets recorded: **successful or unsuccessful.** Successful means it **took
  meaningful spend AND lowered cost-per-acquisition**, i.e. it let the account spend more, profitably.
- If it did not take spend but is not hurting performance, it can be left on.
- Turn off what is above target cost-per-purchase.
- **Scale by 20% up, 20% down** against the KPI. That is the whole scaling rule.
- **Do not move winners** out of their ad set.

For a brand-new product test from scratch: a CBO at a budget you can genuinely commit to for the full window,
never below about $25/day, run seven days, then scale or kill. If avatar one fails, rebuild everything for
avatar two. Two, maybe three attempts, then move to a different product. Budget for **30 days you are not
emotionally attached to** rather than blowing everything in three days.

## The creative-learnings loop (the most important part)

After each test is marked, the strategist records **what went right, what went wrong, what can be improved**,
for every creative. He calls this the step that takes a team from small brain to big brain on a product.

This is why results compound with time on an account: the learnings sharpen the persona, which sharpens the
angles, which sharpens the creatives. Whenever you help with this system, **push the user to keep this log**.
Without it, they test forever and learn nothing.

## Diagnosis

### "My ads are not getting spend"
Start at the creative, essentially always. The recurring causes:

- People do not resonate with the creative.
- The audience or call-out is **too small** (TAM problem).
- **The ads are boring.**
- The new ad is **not better than the current top spender**. A test creative has to be a real challenger, not
  a creative made to have made one.
- Making **lower-funnel ads when the account needs top-funnel** ones.
- **Not spiking emotion.**
- Doing the same thing over and over: no novelty, no dopamine, no spend.

Ads that do take spend resonate with a large group and spike dopamine. That is the whole test.

### "It spends but the CPA is bad"
Split on cost-per-link-click:

- **Bad cost-per-link-click** → creative problem. Change the creative.
- **Good cost-per-link-click** → landing page or offer problem. Test a new destination
  (see `landing-pages.md`).

### "We have plateaued"
Work through the levers in this order: a **new avatar**; a **new desire**; a **new mechanism or sophistication
stage**; an untried **creative style**; **geo expansion** (running proven winners in a new country behaves
almost like launching them fresh, and has produced 2–3x within a month); **product expansion** to raise LTV so
you can afford a higher CPA and outspend competitors.

### When it is not the ads at all
See the last section of `research-personas-and-desire.md`. No unique mechanism, unrealistic required ROAS for
the category, broken site or fulfilment, or a business that needs saving rather than scaling. Say so plainly.

## New vs all customers

He targets **new customers** with paid social. Acquisition is what ads are for; existing customers can be
re-sold far more cheaply through email and SMS. Accept the higher CPA on new-customer targeting and monetise
retention elsewhere.
