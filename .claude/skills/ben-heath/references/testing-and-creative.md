# Testing and creative

## The problem Andromeda created

Meta now suppresses near-duplicate ads: several minor variations of one concept get collapsed, one enters
the auction, the rest get effectively nothing.

But small changes matter enormously. A different text overlay, or a different first three seconds of video,
routinely swings performance. So the platform is blind to precisely the differences advertisers most need
to test.

Do not respond to this by guessing. Guessing which overlay or hook to use is how most advertisers have
reacted, and it is not a professional way to run performance marketing.

## The creative testing tool — the actual answer

At the **ad level**, below the creative section.

- Compares **2-5 versions**, and keeps their delivery genuinely separate so each gets a fair read.
- You choose what share of the budget goes to the test.
- Default duration is 7 days, extendable.
- **Change the comparison metric.** It defaults to something like cost per post engagement, which is close
  to useless. Set it to cost per purchase or cost per lead — the metric you actually care about.
- If Meta warns that the budget or duration will not produce a confident result, believe it: raise the
  budget, extend the window, or both. A test that cannot reach significance is wasted spend.
- Afterwards Meta usually shifts spend to the winner. Verify rather than assume, then turn the loser off.

**When you need it:** fine-grained differences — text overlay, background, colour vs black and white, price
banner, and above all **video hooks**.

**When you do not:** genuinely different ads. An image versus a video, or UGC versus founder-led, are
different enough that Meta treats them separately anyway.

So the sequence is: use normal delivery to find the big winners (which format, which style), then use the
creative testing tool to refine the details of the winner.

## Hook testing is still valid

A widespread claim that the hook method is dead post-Andromeda is wrong. What is true is that two 40-second
videos differing only in their first few seconds may get collapsed into one — so you run them through the
creative testing tool instead.

The efficient production model stands: shoot **one or two full ad bodies**, then produce **many hook
variations** — different opening lines, different visual settings — and you have 20 ads from two shoots.
Test the hooks against each other, then take the winning hook into the main ad set alongside genuinely
different creative.

## Creative diversity, realistically

Meta wants many genuinely different ads: statics, carousels and videos running simultaneously; UGC,
founder-led, demonstration, testimonial. That is what lets it personalise delivery per person and fights
fatigue.

**But do not let "20 completely different creatives" paralyse you.** That standard is unrealistic for many
businesses, and the observable result is people producing nothing at all because the bar feels impossible.

Treat creative production like a diet: **the schedule you can actually sustain beats the perfect one you
abandon.** Launching with four genuinely different creatives plus some variations is fine. A couple of new
ads tested regularly compounds into a large difference over a year.

## When new creative cannot get delivery

A common failure in a consolidated one-campaign-one-ad-set account: Meta keeps spending on the established
winners, new ads get nothing, and the winners slowly fatigue.

This is the main legitimate reason to deviate from the consolidated structure. Options are covered in
`26 - Copy This Meta Ads Testing Strategy` and `35 - Meta advertisers, we've got a TESTING problem` in the
knowledge base. Often you can still test within the existing structure using the creative testing tool with
an allocated share of budget — try that before adding structure.

## What good creative looks like

From his teardowns of real brands:

- **Variety of format and register in the same account** — high-production alongside deliberately rough UGC,
  meme-style statics alongside polished ones. The strongest advertisers span that whole range.
- **Same offer, different pain point.** Two ads to the same landing page, one leading on a freebie and one
  on removing decision fatigue, reach different people. This is what separates the best advertisers from
  the merely competent.
- **Demographic-specific ads** convert well despite being inefficient to produce, because they speak
  directly to one group.
- **Congruence between ad and landing page.** If the product looks light grey in the ad and pure white on
  the page, people bounce, wondering whether they landed in the right place.
- **Send clicks to a dedicated page**, not a giant catch-all page where the product is hard to find.
- **Always give a clear call to action.** Brand-led ads routinely forget to tell people what to do next.
